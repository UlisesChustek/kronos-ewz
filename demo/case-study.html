Interpretation

What transferred — and what didn’t

Separating “fine-tuning changed the marginal output” from “the model forecasts direction” is the whole point of the exercise. The two answers are different.

Changed ✓

  • Marginal scale: point error dropped 18–20% versus zero-shot. This is measured adaptation associated with fine-tuning, not proof of a learned volatility mechanism.
  • Marginal calibration: raw Brier improved from 0.442 to 0.326 versus zero-shot, and validation-fit 90% intervals cover 92.4% of outcomes on average pointwise — though the probability ranking never beats the 0.248 base-rate benchmark.
  • Interval width: a single validation-fit path-scale factor lifts average pointwise coverage from 69.4% to 92.4% (terminal 64.8% → 91.6%). This is calibration, not conditional volatility forecasting.
  • Transfer from pretraining: a 24.7M model adapted to a new asset with ~2k independent days. The evidence is consistent with transfer, but does not isolate it.

Didn’t generalize →

  • Conditional direction: 45.1% at 24 bars, 51.2% at 7 bars — indistinguishable from chance, and identical to always predicting down at 24 bars.
  • Regime drift: a −0.40% mean path drift over 24 bars while the test period drifted +0.16%.
  • Probability ranking: raw p_up is overdispersed; calibration then collapses it toward the 54.9% base rate.
  • Vol expansion: amplification 0.99 predicted vs 1.26 realized; tails remain too thin.

Diagnosis

Why it failed — three explanations, tested

“It doesn’t beat a random walk” is a result, not an explanation. Three candidates explain it: a stale directional bias, the absence of conditional signal, or the wrong evaluation target. Each is separable on the same 71 held-out windows.

1 · The bearish tilt is drift, and drift is removable

The model’s paths drift down by 0.40% on average over 24 bars, while the test period drifted +0.16%. That single offset explains the apparent bearish call — and almost nothing else. Removing it (subtracting the cross-path mean drift per window) gives a clean decomposition:

Forecast variant Mean terminal move Direction AUC P(up) MAE / RW Beats RW
Raw model paths −0.34% 45.1% 0.46 1.074 45.1%
Common drift removed +0.05% 49.3% 0.43 1.027 42.3%
Per-path detrended · shape only +0.03% 43.7% 0.51 1.040 35.2%
Random walk 0.00% — 0.50 1.000 —

Drift removal lifts the median from −0.34% to +0.05% and Brier skill from −0.32 to −0.03, but direction only reaches 49.3%, AUC falls to 0.43, and MAE stays 2.7% worse than a random walk. Per-path detrending — keeping shape, deleting each path’s own trend — lands at AUC 0.51 and 43.7% direction. No directional advantage was demonstrated for the detrended paths in these windows.

Drift explains the tilt, not the error. Correcting it fixes the average level and the probability calibration. These corrections did not demonstrate reliable predictive skill from the last 512 bars to the next 24 on the evaluated sample.

2 · No conditional signal survives a fair baseline

Same features, same chronological split, same windows. Momentum, reversal, and a regularized logistic/ridge model are the cheapest test of whether EWZ itself is predictable at this horizon. At 24 bars, nothing is:

At 24 bars every AUC interval covers 0.50, and Kronos sits at the bottom of the pack. At 7 bars the exploratory logistic fit reached AUC 0.59 (nominal permutation p ≈ 0.01) and 53.7% direction, but this is a retrospective result from repeated inspection of the same test period — not a pre-registered confirmation. It did not improve MAE over a random walk (0.00821 for both), and its direction edge sits inside the always-down baseline’s interval. The later expanding-window v5 benchmark found no material gain either: at 7 bars, logistic terminal MAE was unchanged, direction ≈ 50.3%, and Brier skill ≈ +0.001 (results/walk_forward_baselines_v5.json). The asset is not obviously predictable at five days; at one day any apparent edge is small, fragile, and unconfirmed.

3 · The probability layer is wrong in both directions

Raw

Overdispersed frequencies

Across the 71 windows, raw p_up has standard deviation 0.223. The 64 sampled paths estimate the model's own up-probability. Conditional on a fixed model and input, independent path draws have binomial sampling uncertainty. Variation across different windows also includes changing model probabilities; its size alone establishes neither predictive information nor pure sampling noise. Held-out ranking evidence is weak.

Calibrated

Collapsed toward the base rate

The validation-fit isotonic map shrinks p_up to a standard deviation of 0.037, close to the base-rate-only forecast. Again, this is not independent binomial evidence: the point is that Brier skill stays at −0.03, so calibration corrects the average level but not the ranking.

Conditional hit rates

The signal runs backwards

Descriptively, when raw p_up said up (25 windows), 52.0% actually rose; when it said down (46 windows), 56.5% rose. The logistic slope of “actual up” on logit p_up is −0.53. These are retrospective window counts, not a confirmed predictive relationship.

What the evidence points to

Plausible, partially isolated

  • Objective mismatch: token-level cross-entropy optimizes the conditional density of tokenized OHLC, not the conditional mean of 24-bar returns. Good marginal fit and no mean edge are compatible.
  • Model-side drift: the −0.40% path drift is not inherited from the training set (+0.04% per 24 bars), so it is a sampling/generative bias, not a learned regime call.

Plausible, not isolated

  • Sample size: 71 non-overlapping 5-day windows (~2k effective independent days) give a direction interval of roughly ±11pp. A small edge cannot be ruled out — only a practically useful one.
  • Asset and horizon: one ETF, one 12-month test period, hourly CFD bars. The 7-bar logistic result hints at short-horizon reversal that a 5-day target cannot exploit.

Scope. This is a diagnostic of one model on one asset over one period — not a verdict on Kronos or financial foundation models. It rules out a usable 5-day point edge here. It does not rule out value in cross-sectional ranking, distributional risk, or longer histories.

Engineering

What was actually built

Beyond training: an audit that fixed several data and attention defects, a calibration harness fit only on validation data, and an evaluation stack with confidence intervals.

Bug 01 · data pipeline

Normalization leakage

The repo’s dataset normalized each window with statistics that included future bars, while inference used lookback only. Patched to past_x = x[:lookback] and verified per-sample mean ≈ 0, std ≈ 1.

Bug 02 · inference + training

Causal cross-attention

Single-query cross-attention collapsed RoPE positions during autoregressive decoding, and full-sequence cross-attention was non-causal. The local fix uses causal attention when q_len == seq_len, lets a one-query decode attend to all history, and combines padding masks with the causal mask. Unit tests check that future bars cannot change past outputs.

Evaluation

Calibration without test fitting

Temperature / top-p selection, isotonic maps for p_up, and volatility/path scaling are fit on validation only. However, the test period was inspected repeatedly across diagnostic variants, so all test metrics remain retrospective rather than a fresh holdout.

Statistics

Uncertainty, not point claims

Bootstrap confidence intervals for MAE/RMSE deltas, Wilson intervals for direction, and Brier-skill intervals. The v4 MAE delta CI includes zero: no significant gap.

Method

Reproducible by construction

One chronological split, one evaluation protocol, four ablations. Hyperparameters and calibration are fit on validation, but the test period was inspected repeatedly across variants — so the numbers are retrospective, not a fresh holdout.

Both horizons

The result is not horizon-specific

A 24-bar window on session data is ~5 calendar days, not 24 hours. The shorter 7-bar horizon (~1 trading day) gives the model its best shot. The negative result is consistent across both, though both were evaluated retrospectively on the same inspected history.
